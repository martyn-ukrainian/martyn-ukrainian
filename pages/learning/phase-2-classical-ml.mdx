# Journal — Phase 2: Classical ML

> **Status:** ▶ In progress · ~40 hours in · scikit-learn &amp; XGBoost
>
> This is a living entry — I update it as I move through the phase.

## Goal of this phase

Get genuinely comfortable with classical ML end-to-end: framing a problem,
preparing data, training and tuning a model, and — the part tutorials skip —
knowing *why* a model behaves the way it does and whether to trust it.

## What I've covered so far

- **Regression** — linear &amp; polynomial, regularization (Ridge / Lasso), and
  reading residuals instead of just chasing R².
- **Classification** — logistic regression, k-NN, decision boundaries, and why
  accuracy lies on imbalanced data.
- **Trees &amp; ensembles** — decision trees, random forests, gradient boosting
  with XGBoost. Bias/variance made concrete.
- **The workflow around the model** — train/validation/test discipline,
  cross-validation, leakage traps, and feature scaling.
- **Evaluation** — precision/recall, ROC-AUC, confusion matrices, and picking a
  metric that matches the actual problem.

## Things that finally clicked

- **Regularization is just "don't trust any one feature too much."** Seeing Lasso
  zero out coefficients made it concrete in a way the equations never did.
- **Boosting = learning from your own mistakes, literally.** Each tree fits the
  residual the last one left behind. Explaining that out loud to the voice agent
  is where it locked in.
- **The metric is a product decision, not a math one.** Optimizing accuracy on a
  95/5 split is how you ship a model that predicts "no" every time and looks great.

## Still shaky (being honest)

- Intuition for **when gradient boosting overfits** vs. when to keep adding
  trees — I lean on early stopping but want the feel, not just the callback.
- **Hyperparameter search** beyond grid/random — Bayesian methods are on the list.

## Up next

Wrapping Phase 2 with a small end-to-end project on a real dataset, then into
**Phase 3 — deep learning foundations**: neural nets from scratch before touching
PyTorch.
