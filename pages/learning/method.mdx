# The method

Most self-study fails in the same place: you read, you nod along, you *feel* like
you understood — and a week later it's gone. The fix isn't more input. It's
**retrieval and explanation**. So I built my learning loop around exactly that.

## The loop

1. **Mentor sets the lesson.** Claude Code acts as a mentor — it picks the next
   lesson from the roadmap, explains a concept, and gives me a small hands-on
   task. It has context on what I already know, so it doesn't repeat or skip.
2. **I do the work.** Notebooks, small scripts, real datasets — not just reading.
3. **Voice reflection.** Right after each lesson, a **voice agent in the
   terminal** asks me to explain what I just learned, out loud, for ~10 minutes.
   Speaking exposes the gaps that silent reading hides — the moment I can't
   explain something cleanly, I know I don't actually have it yet.
4. **Spaced retrieval.** Concepts come back on a schedule. The mentor quizzes me
   on older material so it doesn't decay.
5. **Calibrated profile.** My knowledge is tracked as a profile — what I know,
   how confident I am, and where I'm shaky — so the next lesson is always aimed
   at the right edge.

> Explaining out loud catches what reading doesn't. 10 minutes of voice
> reflection after each lesson turns notes into knowledge.

## Why voice?

I work in voice AI, so this is a bit on-brand — but the reason is real. Writing a
summary lets you hide behind copy-paste and half-formed phrases. Speaking in real
time doesn't. If I stumble explaining gradient descent to the agent, that stumble
*is* the signal. It's the same principle as rubber-duck debugging, made
deliberate and spaced.

## Why in public?

The process is the artifact. A finished course tells you I sat through it; an open
journal shows **how I actually learn** — where I struggled, what clicked, how I
recovered. That's far more useful to anyone deciding whether to work with me.

Everything lives in the [claude-mentor repo](https://github.com/martyn-ukrainian/claude-mentor):
roadmap, lessons, retrieval schedule, and the knowledge profile.
